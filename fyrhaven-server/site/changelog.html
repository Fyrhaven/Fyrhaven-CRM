Changelog

What is new on the server

Every change to the service your records live on, newest first. The plugin keeps its own release notes — what changes inside the webmail does not belong here.

Anything a customer has to do about a change is said in the entry. So far, nothing here has needed a migration by hand.

1.16.1 6 November 2026

  • This page had no link preview. Its og:image was a relative .svg, and no crawler resolves a relative path or renders an SVG — so a shared link expanded to nothing. It is an absolute PNG now, with the Twitter spelling beside the Open Graph one, and both are pinned by tests: a broken card fails silently, which is the worst way to fail.

1.16.0 6 November 2026

  • Records can live in a bucket. A workspace's storage is SQLite or any S3-compatible bucket, chosen from the panel's Usage tab.
  • The move is checked both ways. A migration writes every record, reads it back and compares before switching the workspace over; a write that quietly truncates aborts the move and leaves the original provider in place.
  • A bucket that answers with the first page of keys no longer looks complete: the listing follows the continuation token.

1.15.0 6 November 2026

  • The S3 client's contract is pinned by tests — the signature, the paging, the encoding of a key — so a bucket provider cannot change behaviour quietly.

1.14.3 6 November 2026

  • robots.txt and sitemap.xml, so the public pages are indexable and the private ones are not.

1.14.0 5 November 2026

  • An erasure finishes even when the bucket refuses: the record leaves the workspace, and the report names the object left behind instead of failing the whole request.

1.12.0 4 November 2026

  • Prices in more than one currency, stored as prices rather than converted at checkout — a SEK price is a number somebody chose, not a rate.

1.10.0 3 November 2026

  • Moving a licence is a three-step flow with a signed release code, rather than a form that prints the key again and hopes.

1.9.0 3 November 2026

  • Terms, privacy and imprint as real pages, and a comparison that answers the question people actually ask: where does my data live?

This service holds your records encrypted at rest with a key it keeps; the operator can read them. What that means is in the privacy page.
